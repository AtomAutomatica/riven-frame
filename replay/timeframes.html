Divergence on every timeframe.
Which clock pays, and when to sell.

Brandon asked for the bullish divergence across timeframes: 1-minute, 5, 10, 15, 30, hourly. So one detector (a confirmed swing low that undercuts the last swing low while RSI-14 and the MACD histogram both come in higher) was run on all of them, built from the same tape, on 160 stocks from 2022-01-03 to 2026-09-18: 1,182 trading days, 649,178 divergences, every one scored against every ordinary 5-minute bar of the same days (13,984,675 of them). Entry is the close of the bar that confirms the divergence. Nothing is drawn on a bar before it is finished.

The one cut that pays
The 5-minute divergence at the day's low.

54,534 cases on 1,182 days. From the buy, the stock's best high before the close averaged +1.04% against +0.85% for any bar: +0.19 points (95% range +0.16 to +0.21). It reached +2% before the close 13% of the time vs 10% for any bar; +1% inside the hour 17% vs 14%.

On 1-minute candles (25 names, the last 64 days): the divergence at the day's low ran to +2.01% vs +1.36% — +0.64 points, n 1,052. Same shape, faster clock, bigger.

In-sample measurement, gross of costs, on the stock itself. Not forward-validated yet. Small on the stock; a near-the-money same-day call turns +1.7% on the underlying into a multiple. That is the whole point of the vehicle.

1 · Which clock — divergence at the day's low, by timeframe

Faster is better. The 1-minute and 5-minute divergent lows carry it; the 10/15/30 carry less; the hourly divergence at the low is worse than an ordinary bar. The hourly is the wrong clock for this entry (a separate 2-year test of the hourly at the 50-day agreed: −1.65 points).

2 · What subtracts — the same 5-minute divergence with things added

  • Stacking timeframes does not help. A 5-minute divergence while the 15-minute or the hourly is also diverging is worse than the 5-minute alone; three timeframes agreeing is worse still. Agreement on the slow clocks means the slow trend is still down.
  • A double divergence is worse. The second lower low says the seller is still there.
  • Within 1% of the daily 50-day it is worse on every timeframe. The retest-of-the-50 trade and the intraday divergent low are two different trades. Do not size one on the other.
  • Anywhere but the day's low: nothing. The location is the signal, the divergence is the clock.

3 · When to sell — the next day carries nothing

The money is the same-day exit. Buy the divergent low, sell the pop before the close. That is the QQQ 718 call on 9/18: bought 11:49 CT at the 715 low, sold by 14:17. Held to the next day it would have been noise.

5 · Through the vehicle — a real call, real option prices

The stock moving +1.65% is not the trade. The trade is the call. So 2,613 of these at-the-low divergences (the 38 most-traded names, since Feb 2024, when option minute bars begin) were priced through the call one strike above the entry on that week's expiry — same-day expiry for QQQ, SPY and IWM — filled at the close of the confirming 5-minute bar, from that contract's own 5-minute bars, and compared with the same contract bought at every other bar of that day.

  • The odds are real and the exit is everything. A 0DTE call off the divergent low doubles 41% of the time before the close, but held to the bell its median is -85%. The pop must be sold. That is his own law: the exit makes the big day.
  • When the stock reaches +2% the call pays +72% at the median (666 cases, 37% doubled). When the stock never reaches +1% (1,404 cases) the call's median close is -31%. The divergence buys the ticket; the day decides.
  • Fifteen minutes late still works. Filled 15 minutes after the confirm (the live scanner's data lag), mean best high +67% vs +69% on time.
  • Fills are the last trade of the confirming bar, not the ask; spreads on 0DTE near-the-money calls are typically 2–8% of premium and would take that off every number here. Gross of commissions. 208 events had no tradeable contract bars and 179 no trade in the fill window; they are excluded, which flatters liquidity.
