Divergence on every timeframe.
Which clock pays, and when to sell.

Brandon asked for the bullish divergence across timeframes: 1-minute, 5, 10, 15, 30, hourly. So one detector (a confirmed swing low that undercuts the last swing low while RSI-14 and the MACD histogram both come in higher) was run on all of them, built from the same tape, on 160 stocks from 2022-01-03 to 2026-09-18: 1,182 trading days, 649,178 divergences, every one scored against every ordinary 5-minute bar of the same days (13,984,675 of them). Entry is the close of the bar that confirms the divergence. Nothing is drawn on a bar before it is finished.

Correction · Sat 9/19 08:35 — the lift below was the clock, not the divergence
Matched on the time of day, the 5-minute divergence at the day's low has no lift.

Friday's page compared the divergent lows with every ordinary 5-minute bar. But 27% of these divergent lows print in the first hour (only 8% of ordinary bars do), and a first-hour entry has the whole day ahead of it, so its "best high before the close" is larger by construction. Compared with bars from the same session and the same half-hour, the divergent low is slightly worse, not better:

Read the three pictures together: the road falls all day (an 8:45 entry has the whole session to make a high, a 2:45 entry has fifteen minutes); the divergent lows sit on the road at every half-hour, never above it; and they simply happen to print early. Their average was higher only because more of them started higher up the road. Watch one on candles.

Put into the lab's pool with a placebo (random bars matched on session and half-hour), 576 of 994 intraday cells sit below their placebo and only 9 thin cells beat it, none of them stable across time windows. What survives: the divergence still marks a low and the next day still carries nothing (sell the same day), the 15-minute lag still costs nothing, and the option odds in section 5 are real odds for morning near-the-money calls — but none of it is evidence that the divergence itself adds edge. Sizing comes from the catalyst and the frame, not from the divergence. Friday's charts and tables are kept below, unchanged, so the mistake stays visible.

The 1-minute version (25 names, 65 days, 1,048 divergent lows): +2.01% vs +1.37% for any bar (Friday's "+0.64 points") becomes +0.10 points against the same session and half-hour — 23% of those events print in the first half-hour. Same story, a small residual that the pool would need to test.

Method of the correction: the same 54,181 events, baseline bars grouped by (session, 30-minute bucket) and by (symbol, session, bucket); lab family intraday with a clock-matched placebo per study, BH q=0.10, six stability windows.

1 · Which clock — divergence at the day's low, by timeframe

Read with the correction above: these bars compare each divergence with any bar. The faster clocks print earlier in the day, which is where the apparent lift came from; matched on the clock, none of them carries it. The hourly divergence at the low remains worse than an ordinary bar either way.

2 · What subtracts — the same 5-minute divergence with things added

  • Stacking timeframes does not help. A 5-minute divergence while the 15-minute or the hourly is also diverging is worse than the 5-minute alone; three timeframes agreeing is worse still. Agreement on the slow clocks means the slow trend is still down.
  • A double divergence is worse. The second lower low says the seller is still there.
  • Within 1% of the daily 50-day it is worse on every timeframe. The retest-of-the-50 trade and the intraday divergent low are two different trades. Do not size one on the other.
  • Anywhere but the day's low: nothing. And at the day's low, nothing once the clock is matched (see the correction).

3 · When to sell — the next day carries nothing

The same-day exit still stands — holding a morning entry overnight carries nothing. That is the QQQ 718 call on 9/18: bought 11:49 CT at the 715 low, sold by 14:17. Held to the next day it would have been noise.

5 · Through the vehicle — a real call, real option prices

The stock moving +1.65% is not the trade. The trade is the call. So 2,613 of these at-the-low divergences (the 38 most-traded names, since Feb 2024, when option minute bars begin) were priced through the call one strike above the entry on that week's expiry — same-day expiry for QQQ, SPY and IWM — filled at the close of the confirming 5-minute bar, from that contract's own 5-minute bars, and compared with the same contract bought at every other bar of that day.

  • The odds are real and the exit is everything. A 0DTE call off the divergent low doubles 41% of the time before the close, but held to the bell its median is -85%. The pop must be sold. That is his own law: the exit makes the big day.
  • When the stock reaches +2% the call pays +72% at the median (666 cases, 37% doubled). When the stock never reaches +1% (1,404 cases) the call's median close is -31%. The divergence buys the ticket; the day decides.
  • Caveat (9/19): the paired comparison here is against the same contract at every other bar of the day, so it carries the same time-of-day composition as the stock-level lift; the odds are real odds for morning near-the-money calls, not proof the divergence adds anything. Fifteen minutes late still works. Filled 15 minutes after the confirm (the live scanner's data lag), mean best high +67% vs +69% on time.
  • Fills are the last trade of the confirming bar, not the ask; spreads on 0DTE near-the-money calls are typically 2–8% of premium and would take that off every number here. Gross of commissions. 208 events had no tradeable contract bars and 179 no trade in the fill window; they are excluded, which flatters liquidity.
