What the machine measured today

In one line: the option spread kills 93% of what looked like a winner, pair reversion is real but does not pay the toll, bursts of trading mark the wild minutes but not the direction, cheap implied vol is the market being right, and half of what survives the pool is the same bet counted twice.

1 · The option crossing kills 93% of the "winners"

157,696 option-payoff cells were re-run charging the real spread from our own quote tape. Of the 67,237 that looked positive before costs, 4,720 are still positive after. The direction was never the problem; the crossing is.

Where the survivors sit, by strike and by holding period:

The same cells inside the frames: the risk-on side keeps two to three times more net winners than the risk-off side. The frame is a filter on when option buying pays at all.

Most of what survives is 20% out of the money held one to three days. That is a lottery ticket whose average is carried by rare huge wins, not a rule. The at-the-money survivors are the 207 cells worth a forward count.

The sequence itself, all stocks: every instance of the COIN sequence (3,974 across every name) lined up at the retest and played forward, COIN on top → the sequential composite.

2 · Pairs: the rubber band is real, the toll eats it

MARA/RIOT, spread z-score over the last 250 sessions (60-session window). Dots = a 2-sigma stretch; the number is the spread's move over the next 5 sessions, green = it snapped back.

Across all 2,507 pairs (bitcoin stocks and every name's three closest cousins): after a 2-sigma stretch the spread comes back +0.09 points on average vs random days, and -0.10 after a 0.10% crossing per leg. Real, and not worth the trip at this size.

The one cell that stands out: bitcoin-stock pairs while BTC is over its 50-week. 157 stretches on 59 sessions, 63% came back. But those 5-day windows overlap, so it is about a dozen independent weeks, and the frame is off today (BTC is under its 50-week). It is in the pool; the forward ledger scores it when the frame turns on.

Reversion pays only on a calm, risk-on tape. In a storm or under the 200 the stretch keeps stretching.

2b · The 1-minute tape, all 160 names: still the clock

Your Friday ask, finished tonight on the full 1-minute tape since 2022 (2.0 million divergences, 80 million baseline bars). At the session low the 1-minute divergence earns +1.05 points to the close; random bars on the same sessions and half-hours earn +1.05. The divergence itself is worth about 0.01 points over its clock. The 5-minute cut rebuilt from 1-minute bars gives the same answer as Friday's 5-minute tape: below its placebo. The timeframe does not rescue it. 960 cells are in the pool with the matched placebo.

3 · Bursts of trading mark the wild minutes, not the direction

When trades cluster on themselves (one print causing the next) and the last five minutes moved one way, the next half hour on average gives back about a tenth of a percent, in every part of the session (all 160 names, 1-minute bars since 2022; not one of the 192 continuation cells beat its same-clock placebo). The only thing that does beat the placebo is the size of the move in either direction. That is volatility, not a direction. A burst is a fade at best, never a chase.

4 · Cheap implied vol: the market was right

On the 29 names with an option tape, buying the at-the-money call when its implied vol was cheapest against realized paid 3.13% of spot over ten sessions; names with the same volatility on the same dates paid 3.95%. Cheap insurance was the market correctly pricing volatility about to fall. 1,655 cases on 440 dates.

5 · Half of what survives is the same bet twice

These are the 16 signals that survived the pool for one question (5-day rank, all names). A line means the two signals pick the same names on the same days more than random would (12% overlap vs 5% for random picks). Joined signals are one bet. The gold cluster, the whole trend axis, is one bet carrying a quarter of the rows. 16 signals = 8 bets.

Across every cell answered so far, breadth is a third to a half of the survivor count.

Why it matters: a small edge pays only across many independent bets. Counting rows overstated our breadth two to three times.

6 · The forward ledger is running

Every one of the 142,566 surviving signals is now frozen and scored on every new session with the same arithmetic that found it. On the seed window (the last 20 sessions, which overlap the fit) 59.5% agreed in sign, but session by session it is a coin flip. The real out-of-sample count starts Monday, weekdays at 4:45.

7 · Your cell legs added nothing to the model

Run 3 added the seven legs of the cell (a year under the 50, breakout age, first retest) to the model. Same accuracy, same lift, same top five features. Distance from the high and from the 200 already carried what the legs say.

8 · The lab now writes its own next questions

Of 2,880,829 finished studies the direction families clear the q=0.10 pool at 14 to 27 percent — btc_frame 167 of 1,232 (13.6), frame_x 358 of 2,464 (14.5), ic 4,210 of 17,248 (24.4), cond_ic 14,626 of 54,450 (26.9) — while the payoff families clear at 62 to 91 percent (payoff 22,367 of 24,640, or 90.8), a gap near 66 percentage points that says more about the payoff statistic's null than about money. The survivors are far fewer than the rows suggest: 167 surviving btc_frame rows collapse to 22 distinct signals and 59,169 cond_wide rows collapse to 56, and the same handful of features — sma50_over_sma200, close_over_sma200, under50_252/126/504, roc120, pct_of_52w_high — holds the top effect in every family, so the pool is restating one long-trend axis and one drawdown axis across slices. Effects stay small and frame-dependent, best correlations running about +0.05 to +0.07 unconditioned and +0.14 to +0.28 once a universe or regime is stacked on, and only 4,128 finished studies — 0.14 percent of the total — have been costed against a quoted spread, with intraday still the thinnest family at 994 done and 218 refused.

Twice a day a research pass reads the pool and queues the next studies itself, inside the same grammar and the same false-discovery pool. First pass: 40 proposed, 32 queued, 8 refused for inventing a regime that does not exist. Its own first proposal, the crowding picture above, was built the same afternoon. Next on its list:

  • regime_flip_timing — Does a regime-conditioned survivor still pay when the regime label is lagged to when it was actually knowable, and how much of its effect sits in the first ten days after a regime flip?
  • forward_since_registration — Do survivors pay in the window after the date they first cleared the pool, and at what rate does the measured effect shrink between the in-registry sample and the post-registration sample?
  • name_jackknife — How much of each family's survival is carried by a handful of names or months — does the survivor count hold when the top five percent of contributing names, or the three best calendar months, are dropped?
